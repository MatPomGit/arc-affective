\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
A synthetic stream can test queue discipline and accounting but cannot establish sensor latency, tracking quality or model accuracy. Software-injected service times do not replicate actual GPU/CPU inference, PCIe transfer or vendor SDK behavior. Cross-device timestamps are not comparable solely because both are expressed as nanoseconds. Runtime frequency scaling, OS scheduling and background tasks introduce additional uncertainty.

The broader scheduling literature includes deadline and adaptive stream controllers \cite{nigade2024,sedlak2025,dynamicbatch2024}; a new paper requires a measurable differentiated mechanism and controlled comparison against these baselines. If no improvement survives real-node replication, report that outcome and narrow the engineering claim rather than retroactively selecting a different primary metric.

\section{Conclusion}
This methods-stage manuscript defines an auditable performance and quality trade-off for multimodal edge scheduling under faults. Publication readiness depends on a frozen protocol, real-clock evaluation on actual hardware, comparative implementation and independent reproducibility.
